The story

Berrin is a fictional inland New South Wales town: a river, a cotton gin, a closed abattoir, a bowls club turned youth drop-in centre. Mara Bell drives the van that returns young people to safe places. The rules are clear when printed.

The novel follows workers, families and young people whose lives meet around a community youth service. It asks what care, responsibility and accountability look like when institutional procedures meet urgent human needs, and who is left holding the gap between a recorded outcome and what happened.

About the Author

Sergey Alexeev is a Senior Research Associate at UNSW’s Nura Gili Centre for Indigenous Programs and an Adjunct Senior Lecturer at the Sydney Clinical Trials Centre, University of Sydney.

He is an Associate Editor of the International Journal of Mental Health Systems and a member of the Editorial Board of the International Journal of Drug Policy.

Safe Return is his first novel.

Safe Return is a personal creative project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by UNSW or the University of Sydney.

More about his research at alexeev.pw

How the Novel Came Together

Safe Return grew out of my research, publications, research notes and professional experience across Australian health, justice, public policy and community services, including co-design and community-engaged research.

I developed the novel under my creative direction, bringing together observations and material from fields that are usually studied separately. Generative AI tools, including ChatGPT and Claude, helped synthesize this material and assisted with drafting and editing the prose.

I directed the story’s development and undertook substantial editorial review and revision.

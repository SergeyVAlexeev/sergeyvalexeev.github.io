About

I am a Senior Research Associate at UNSW’s Nura Gili Centre for Indigenous Programs and an Adjunct Senior Lecturer at the University of Sydney’s NHMRC Clinical Trials Centre. I serve as an Associate Editor of the International Journal of Mental Health Systems and as a member of the Editorial Board of the International Journal of Drug Policy, and contribute to the Institute for Replication (I4R). I’m of Ingrian-Finn heritage.ⓘ Ingrian Finns are a Finnish-speaking minority historically rooted in Ingria (around St Petersburg). Many families experienced displacement and assimilation pressures across the 20th century.

  • Why twelve notes? An economic model of scale size and evidence on chromatic pitch-class use in Western classical music, 1485–1963 We model musical scale size as a trade-off between harmonic gains and construction and learning costs, and show with 623 classical compositions that chromatic pitch use expanded historically before saturating within a twelve-note palette.

Research program & grants

  • REVALUE-AU: Revaluing Work and Evaluating Labour-market Adjustment in Australia REVALUE-AU will study what happens after Australian tribunals decide that work done mainly by women, in aged care, disability and community services, childcare and dental assisting, has been undervalued, and order its legal pay rate to rise. Using worker-level data, we track what those decisions actually change: wages, hours, employment, retention, and how employers, funders and services respond. The aim is evidence on what wage correction does once it reaches the payroll.
    Webpage · Project spine · One-pager
  • EVIDENCE: Evaluating Interventions for Drug Epidemics—Net Community Effectiveness This project will study how harm reduction programs—such as safe injection centres and needle exchanges—in Sydney and Melbourne affect health, safety, and wellbeing for everyone. We aim to find out what works best, for whom, and how to make drug policies fairer and more effective across Australia. All results will be openly shared to help communities, service providers, and policymakers make better decisions. Special focus will be given to young people and First Nations Australians.
    Proposal under NHMRC Investigator grant · Latest feedback
  • HOPE: Health Outcomes and Post-incarceration Evaluation The HOPE project will find out how going to prison affects people’s health in Australia. We compare people sent to prison with those given community orders, using a research method that shows the true impact of prison. By working with Aboriginal leaders and people with lived experience, we aim to give clear answers to help improve health and justice policies. This evidence is urgently needed to address health gaps and support better outcomes for individuals, families, communities and the nation.
    Webpage · Proposal under EMC MRFF · Latest feedback
  • FAMILY: Safe family-aware early identification and service planning for youth depression FAMILY will find out which aspects of family life—like a parent's mental health or financial stress—can help identify young people at risk of depression, and how services can use that information safely, without stigma or blame. Using national survey and linked data, and working closely with young people, carers and clinicians, we aim to build a practical, ethical framework services can use for earlier, more effective support.
    Webpage · Project spine
  • Robust inference for stepped-wedge trials: addressing negative weights with CARE-SW This project will improve how stepped-wedge cluster trials are analysed when standard estimators can place misleading negative weights on treatment effects. We will develop robust inference tools that remain reliable under staggered rollout, treatment-effect heterogeneity, and practical departures from ideal design assumptions, so trialists and policymakers can make better decisions from complex real-world evidence.
    Proposal under NHMRC Ideas grant

Collaboration & contact

I’m actively looking for new collaborators: co-authors, HDR/PhD candidates, applied partners, and people with decision-facing questions that need careful empirical work. My work spans econometrics, biostatistics, epidemiology, and health economics.

For students, collaboration can include mentorship and supervision: concrete projects can often be shaped into publishable theses or papers. For other collaborators, I’m especially interested in projects where strong design, linked administrative or clinical data, and a clear decision point come together.

If you’re interested in collaborating, email me at sergei.v.alexeev@gmail.com with a short note about your background, interests, and the question you’d like to work on, or message me on WhatsApp at +61 457 871 540. If you’re not quite ready to reach out, you can start with my Sergey AI chat to get quick clarification on my background, interests, and collaboration/supervision approach before contacting me. You’re also welcome to reach out to my current and past students for candid feedback on my supervision style (e.g., Alexandra Gallagher, Jessica Cornish, Oscar Ninou, and Rupa Mannan).

For prospective HDR/PhD students

  • Scholarships: domestic candidates are typically much less competitive than international candidates (roughly ~70% vs ~10–15%, depending on round and university).
  • Eligibility / competitiveness: Honours degree or ~1 year of research in a Master’s with strong grades (≈75%+); otherwise, first-author papers plus ~1 year FTE research experience.
  • Clear evidence of strong English language capability.
